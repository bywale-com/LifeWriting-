## I. The definition that opened this file

The working definition, recorded in the founder’s wording:

> Discovery = acquiring previously unavailable information about the situation by perceiving, connecting, and interrogating signals in the environment.

That information can be about people. It does not have to be.

For social asymmetry-seeking, Discovery was listed as including:

- noticing anomalies,
- detecting relevance between events,
- reading behavior,
- recognizing timing,
- noticing omissions,
- noticing changes from baseline,
- identifying incongruence,
- tracking who reacts to whom,
- inferring hidden variables,
- seeing power structure,
- identifying incentives,
- noticing constraints,
- tracking relational movement,
- spotting what is being concealed,
- detecting what produces emotional movement,
- noticing what someone consistently rewards or punishes.

That list already spans several fields. It is not a body-language list.

The research question used as the brief:

> What established disciplines, techniques, and training methods improve the ability to detect, connect, interpret, and test meaningful signals in complex social environments?

Searched across: observational skill, situational awareness, investigative interviewing, intelligence analysis, clinical interviewing, behavioral science, nonverbal communication, negotiation, deception research, social cognition, pattern recognition, and hypothesis testing.

The scaffold that must stay uncollapsed:

> Detection — notice that something happened.  
> Relevance linking — identify what it may be connected to.  
> Interpretation — generate plausible meanings.  
> Hypothesis formation — hold candidate explanations.  
> Discrimination — identify what evidence would separate them.  
> Probe — deliberately acquire more information.  
> Verification — test without assuming the answer.  
> Update — revise the model.

The explicit instruction to the researcher:

> Do not collapse Discovery into body language.

Because the Jane/Lisbon case barely depends on a face. The move is:

> event happened → Lisbon reacted immediately afterward → connect the two → probe.

That is temporal pattern recognition and contextual inference.

The core, compressed:

> Notice more. Connect better. Interpret cautiously. Test intelligently. Update quickly.

## II. Placement in the existing architecture

Discovery is not a new temperament. It is the information engine that Asymmetry-Seeking already assumed.

The previous stack ended:

«Self-Telemetry → Alarm Configuration → Cascade Decoding → Consequence Breakpoint → Relationship Spectrum → Environment Temperature → Aim → Safety/Threat Cues → Asymmetry-Seeking → Strategy → Verification → Learning»

Asymmetry-seeking was defined as using outwardly gathered information to improve the predictive model of the relational system. Discovery is how that information is acquired and made trustworthy.

The earlier taxonomy already named pieces of this:

- Outward Attention, Social Perception, Social Telemetry — Detection.
- Pattern Recognition, Pattern Accumulation — Relevance linking.
- Bounded Inference — Interpretation.
- Signal → Model → Usable Implication — Hypothesis formation.
- Bait — a Probe that is also a Discrimination device.
- State Verification, Aim → Strategy → Predicted Indicator → Observe → Update — Verification and Update.
- Model Updating — Update.

What was missing was the discipline that keeps those pieces from collapsing into “reading people.”

**[Inference]** Discovery is a staged epistemic loop. Asymmetry-seeking is the motive and the reward that keep the loop outward. Relational regulation is a later, separate act: alter the state after the model is good enough.

The debugging analogy from the 25 September note still holds. The same signal can become self-threat, neutral information, or usable asymmetry. Discovery is the machinery that makes the third option possible without turning the first two into a personality.

## III. Why the stages must stay separate

If the stages blur, three failures appear immediately.

**Collapse 1 — Detection becomes Interpretation.**  
Something is noticed and named in the same breath. “She looked uncomfortable” is already a story. The observation was a pause, a topic change, or a glance at the door.

**Collapse 2 — Interpretation becomes Verification.**  
A plausible meaning is treated as confirmed because it is coherent. This is the narrative fallacy applied to a room.

**Collapse 3 — Probe becomes contamination.**  
The question that was supposed to test a hypothesis instead plants it. The person then produces the answer the question required.

The Jane/Lisbon simulation in the previous note already used the stages without naming them:

1. Detection — Lisbon produced a comment after a socially exposing event.
2. Relevance linking — the comment is a candidate second to that event, not ambient chatter.
3. Interpretation — concern, contempt, performance, or something else.
4. Hypothesis formation — Branch A (alignment) and Branch B (concealment) were both live.
5. Discrimination — a reveal of protective orientation would split A from B; repeated deflection would not specify content, only non-disclosure.
6. Probe — “Why do you say that?” then a bait hypothesis.
7. Verification — the reveal, the correction, or the continued refusal.
8. Update — safety information, concealment as anomaly relative to Deep Blue, or unresolved private meaning.

Bait was already defined as hypothesis testing, not manipulation. This file keeps that.

## IV. Do not collapse Discovery into body language

This is a bound, not a preference.

**[Evidence]** Bond and DePaulo (2006), *Personality and Social Psychology Review* 10(3): 206 documents, 24,483 judges. Unaided lie/truth judgments average **54%** correct (47% of lies, 61% of truths). People are better on audible than visible lies. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16859438/

**[Evidence]** DePaulo, Lindsay, Malone, Muhlenbruck, Charlton, and Cooper (2003), “Cues to deception,” *Psychological Bulletin* 129: 1,338 estimates of 158 cues. Median absolute effect is about **d = .10**. Many stereotyped cues (gaze aversion, fidgeting) do not reliably distinguish. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.129.1.74

**[Evidence]** Hartwig and Bond (2011), “Why do lie-catchers fail?,” *Psychological Bulletin* 137. A lens-model meta-analysis: observers roughly use the weak cues that exist. Accuracy is capped because **behavioral differences are weak**, not mainly because people studied the wrong checklist. Training a face-dictionary therefore cannot produce a people-reader. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023589

**[Evidence]** Porter and ten Brinke (2008), *Psychological Science* 19: in 697 coded expressions, microexpressions appeared in about **2%** of expressions, often only in part of the face, and not only in deceivers. Burgoon (2018), *Frontiers in Psychology*: microexpressions are not an operational lie-detection method. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01672/full

**[Evidence]** Barrett, Adolphs, Marsella, Martinez, and Pollak (2019), “Emotional expressions reconsidered,” *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*: hypothesized face configurations (scowl = anger, and so on) show weak reliability and limited generalizability. Context dominates. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6640856/

**[Evidence]** Jordan, Brimbal, Wallace, Kassin, Hartwig, and Street: METT microexpression training performed no better than bogus training or no training on lie/truth judgments. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jip.1532

**[Inference]** Nonverbal research licenses, at most, three weak uses: a change from *this* person’s baseline in *this* setting; timing locked to a specific event or question; cross-channel incongruence as a flag to generate another hypothesis. None of those is Verification. All three are Detection or Relevance-linking cues with low diagnosticity until tested.

**[Inference]** The Jane/Lisbon move does not need a facial coding scheme. It needs a dual-channel watch (event and Lisbon), a contingency test (is B more likely given A than given ~A), and a probe that does not invent the answer.

Mentalism is treated the same way. The transferable residue is observation, base-rate inference, elicitation, treating a reaction as a test, and updating. Forcing, equivoque, cold reading, hot reading sold as intuition, and multiple outs are stagecraft. They guarantee an outcome or conceal misses. That is the opposite of Discovery.

**[Evidence]** Derren Brown has said for years that the act is a mixture of magic, suggestion, psychology, misdirection, and showmanship, and that he would rather a fan take a psychology course than an NLP course. He has used cold reading to expose psychics, not as a billed method of knowledge. (Magic Café interview, 2004; *Tricks of the Mind*, 2006; *New Yorker*, 2019.)

NLP is not a Discovery training. Sharpley (1984, 1987) and Witkowski (2010) reviewed the experimental literature and found predicate-matching and representational-system claims unsupported.

## V. The eight stages

### 1. Detection — notice that something happened

**What it is.** Perception that an element is present, changed, omitted, or newly timed. Not yet what it means.

**What it is not.** “She is hiding something.” That is already Interpretation.

**Home disciplines.** Situational awareness (Endsley Level 1), attention science, ethology, visual observation training.

**[Evidence]** Endsley (1995), “Toward a Theory of Situation Awareness in Dynamic Systems,” *Human Factors* 37(1): SA is perception of elements in a volume of time and space, comprehension of their meaning, and projection of near-future status. Level 1 is monitoring, cue detection, simple recognition. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1518/001872095779049543

**[Evidence]** Jones and Endsley (1996), *Aviation, Space, and Environmental Medicine* 67(6): of SA errors in ASRS reports, **76.3%** were Level 1, **20.3%** Level 2, **3.4%** Level 3. The largest single bucket was failure to monitor or observe available data. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8827130/

**[Inference]** The Jane move dies most often at Detection: Lisbon was not in the scan when A happened, or A was not held long enough to notice B. Misreading a face is the less common failure.

**[Evidence]** Mack and Rock (1998), *Inattentional Blindness*; Simons and Chabris (1999), “Gorillas in Our Midst,” *Perception* 28: a large fraction of observers miss a fully visible unexpected event when attention is locked on another task. Detection rose with task ease and visual similarity of the unexpected object to the attended set. https://chabris.com/Simons1999.pdf

**[Evidence]** Rensink, O’Regan, and Clark (1997), *Psychological Science* 8: large scene changes go unseen when transients are masked. Attention is required to perceive change. A verbal cue and named importance speed detection.

**[Evidence]** Green and Swets (1966), *Signal Detection Theory and Psychophysics*: detection is sensitivity (d′) plus criterion. Raising hits without raising d′ raises false alarms.

**[Evidence]** Altmann (1974), “Observational Study of Behavior: Sampling Methods,” *Behaviour* 49: sampling method *is* the bias. Focal-animal sampling is best for rates, durations, and sequences. Ad libitum sampling is biased toward the dramatic. Sequence sampling captures sequential constraints.

**[Evidence]** Dolev, Friedlaender, and Braverman (2001), *JAMA* 286(9): first-year medical students given a structured session looking at narrative paintings improved detection of clinical visual details by about **10%** versus controls. https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/1031468 · https://news.yale.edu/2001/09/04/artwork-can-sharpen-medical-diagnostic-skills-yale-researchers-report

**[Evidence]** Naghshineh et al. (2008), Harvard/Brigham *Training the Eye*, *Journal of General Internal Medicine*: eight paired art and physical-diagnosis sessions using Visual Thinking Strategies produced +5.41 accurate observations versus +0.36 in controls, with a dose-response at eight or more sessions. VTS splits looking from interpreting: “What is going on? What do you see that makes you say that? What more can you find?” https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-008-0667-0

**Training that actually moves Detection.** Goal-directed scan habits; simulation with freeze-probes (SAGAT) scored against ground truth; art-looking drills that force inventory before story; ethogram plus reliability-to-criterion on tape; focused-attention then open-monitoring practice so attention can stay outward without rumination. Lecture-only SA training is weak. Lee et al. (2017) found simulation beat lecture on SAGAT Perception in a small RCT of critical-care fellows; Comprehension and Projection did not differ.

**Failure modes.** Attentional narrowing. Ad libitum “noticing talent.” Liberal criterion dressed up as perceptiveness. Looking at the speaker of A and missing the Lisbon-channel. Treating activation itself as the signal (that is Self-Telemetry, a different channel).

**Jane mapping.** Dual-channel watch: the event stream and the relevant person. An omission is a Detection, not yet a motive.

### 2. Relevance linking — identify what it may be connected to

**What it is.** Treating two or more detections as a candidate unit: B may be a response to A; C may belong with B; this silence may be a missing second.

**What it is not.** “She is angry at me because of the event.” That adds a mind and a cause.

**Home disciplines.** Endsley Level 2, associative contingency, conversation analysis, ethological sequence sampling, Brunswik’s lens model, Boyd’s Orient.

**[Evidence]** Endsley (1995): Level 2 is synthesis of disjoint Level 1 elements via pattern recognition and a mental model. That is linking, not mind-reading.

**[Evidence]** Rescorla (1967), “Pavlovian Conditioning and Its Proper Control Procedures,” *Psychological Review* 74: pairing is not enough. Learning tracks **contingency**: P(outcome|cue) versus P(outcome|~cue). The truly-random control produces chance pairings and no association. Rescorla (1968) showed that when those two probabilities are equal, there is no conditioning even with many pairings.

**[Inference]** “Lisbon moved after the name” is contiguity. The link is valid only if she is less likely to make that move when the name is absent. One A-then-B is a pairing. This is the formalization of Relevance linking.

**[Evidence]** Watson (1997), “Contingency and Its Two Indices,” *The Behavior Analyst* 20: humans compute a forward index (does B follow A?) and a backward index (was B preceded by A?). Infants detect action–effect contingencies inside a short window; longer delays collapse the raw association. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2733560/

**[Inference]** “Immediately afterward” in the Jane/Lisbon case is not style. It is the temporal window in which a raw contingency is easiest to register. The later probe tests the backward index: was that reaction about this event?

**[Evidence]** Schegloff (1968), “Sequencing in Conversational Openings,” *American Anthropologist* 70: a mere next event is not a sequence. Sequences are bound by **conditional relevance**: a first makes a second due; its absence is noticeable and inferentially loaded. Schegloff and Sacks (1973); Sacks, Schegloff, and Jefferson (1974), *Language* 50: adjacency pairs, next-turn proof procedure. The second speaker’s action displays how the first was understood. Heritage (1984): the working question is “why that now?”

**[Inference]** The pair need not be verbal. An event in the room can function as a first; Lisbon’s next action, delay, or silence is inspectable as a second. Conversation analysis gives a method for tracking who reacts to whom. It does not give motive.

**[Evidence]** Boyd (1995/96), “The Essence of Winning and Losing”: Orientation — genetic heritage, cultural traditions, previous experience, new information, analysis and synthesis — shapes observation. The loop is “an ongoing many-sided implicit cross-referencing process of projection, empathy, correlation, and rejection.” Popular “cycle faster” is a later slogan. https://ooda.de/media/john_boyd_-_the_essence_of_winning_and_losing.pdf

Boyd is already in Pool A. The usable piece here is Orient as a correlation filter, not as combat tempo. Empathy in Boyd’s sketch is perspective-taking for correlation, not affect-reading.

**[Evidence]** Klein, Calderwood, and Clinton-Cirocco (1985/2010), fireground commanders: most decision points were recognition of a typical situation, not option comparison. Klein (1998), *Sources of Power*: expertise is a library of situation shapes; expectancy violation is as diagnostic as a positive match.

**[Evidence]** Chase and Simon (1973), “Perception in Chess,” *Cognitive Psychology* 4: masters reconstruct meaningful positions after five seconds far better than novices; the advantage vanishes on random boards. Chunks are domain-specific.

**[Inference]** Jane looks fast because Lisbon’s ethogram is already in the library. “This shape: event, then this person’s reaction” is a chunk. It does not transfer to a stranger. The taxonomy already said this: he is not solving everything from zero; he recognizes shapes.

**Training.** Sequence sampling on tape. “Why that now?” transcription drills. Cognitive feedback that shows which cues actually predict, not outcome-only “right/wrong” (Hammond’s lens-model line: outcome feedback can reduce policy consistency). Varied exemplars of A→B in one ecology, not a lecture titled “look for reactions.”

**Failure modes.** Contiguity treated as contingency. Ad libitum overfitting of dramatic pairs. Fast OODA with a stale orientation. Importing chess or fireground chunks into a social field where they were never earned.

### 3. Interpretation — generate plausible meanings

**What it is.** Candidate readings of a linked cluster. Meanings are plural and held lightly.

**What it is not.** Closing the case. A single-trait story. “I read the room.”

**Home disciplines.** Intelligence analysis of perception, social cognition, Goffman, clinical process-versus-content.

**[Evidence]** Heuer, *Psychology of Intelligence Analysis* (CIA, 1999): we perceive what we expect; mind-sets form fast and resist change; new data are assimilated to existing images; early exposure to ambiguous stimuli impairs later seeing even after the image clarifies. After a minimum threshold, more collection often raises confidence without raising accuracy. https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/9a5f1162fd0932c29bfed1c030edf4ae/Pyschology-of-Intelligence-Analysis.pdf

**[Evidence]** US IC, *A Tradecraft Primer: Structured Analytic Techniques* (2009): Key Assumptions Check makes the interpretive frame visible; Quality of Information Check tests source soundness; Indicators / Signposts of Change pre-commit to observables that would mark a shift. https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf

**[Evidence]** Jones and Harris (1967); Ross (1977) fundamental attribution error; Gilbert and Malone (1995) correspondence bias: observers infer disposition even when the situation assigned the behavior. System 1 names a trait. System 2 has to hold it open.

**[Evidence]** Kelley (1967, 1973): covariation — consensus, distinctiveness, consistency. High consensus + high distinctiveness + high consistency points to the situation; low consensus + low distinctiveness + high consistency points to the person. A single outburst is non-diagnostic.

**[Evidence]** Goffman, *The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life* (1959): front/backstage, given versus given-off, face-work. Displays are performances under constraint, not windows onto a true self.

**[Evidence]** Safran, Muran, and Eubanks’s Alliance-Focused Training: therapists miss ruptures; missed ruptures predict dropout. Process (how we are relating) is a different channel from content (the story being told). Withdrawal ruptures include false compliance, topic death, thinning affect — omissions as data.

**[Inference]** Interpretation should always produce at least one situational or incentive reading before any trait claim. “What performance is this? Who would be punished for saying otherwise? What is the interaction doing while the words happen?”

**Failure modes.** Correspondence bias. Early blur treated as the object (Heuer). Impression-management literacy used as a cynicism machine. Clinical process language used to diagnose strangers.

### 4. Hypothesis formation — hold candidate explanations

**What it is.** A set of live explanations, including noise. Missing evidence is part of generation: if H were true, what should already have been seen?

**What it is not.** The first story that fits. A confession planned in advance.

**Home disciplines.** Analysis of Competing Hypotheses, Allison’s three models, Wason / confirmation-bias literature, clinical not-knowing.

**[Evidence]** Heuer (1999), Chapter 8, ACH: identify all plausible hypotheses; list evidence; matrix hypotheses × evidence; score diagnosticity; **disprove rather than confirm**; report relative likelihoods; pre-specify what would force revision. The most probable hypothesis is usually the one with the **least evidence against it**.

**[Evidence]** Wason (1960), “On the failure to eliminate hypotheses,” *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology*: intelligent adults test the 2-4-6 rule with confirming triples and announce a rule narrower than the truth. Nickerson (1998), *Review of General Psychology* 2(2): confirmation bias corrupts search, interpretation, and memory.

**[Evidence]** Allison, *Essence of Decision* (1971; 2nd ed. with Zelikow, 1999): do not form a single “rational actor wanted X.” Generate at least three: unitary rational actor, organizational process / SOP, bureaucratic politics. Jervis, *Perception and Misperception in International Politics* (1976): actors misread others’ constraints and update too little when evidence is diagnostic of an alternative type.

**[Evidence]** Fonagy / Bateman, Mentalization-Based Treatment manuals: the stance is inquisitive and **not-knowing**. Prefer “what” over “why.” Formulation is a joint, revisable sketch. Knowing-too-soon is iatrogenic. This is a treatment model with outcome evidence for BPD, not a forensic fact-finding method. The transferable norm is: hidden states stay hypotheses.

**[Inference]** Minimum set for a social signal: (a) disposition, (b) situation or role, (c) incentive or constraint, (d) noise or coincidence. For the Lisbon comment, that is already Branch A, Branch B, performance for the room, and chance timing.

**[Inference]** Later RCTs on ACH-as-matrix are unkind. Dhami, Belton, Mandel and follow-ons: ACH helps listing alternatives but does not reliably improve accuracy and can worsen coherence. The **norm** (write rivals; hunt disconfirmers) is the Discovery habit. The stationery is optional.

**Failure modes.** Satisficing. Taleb’s narrative fallacy: a coherent after-the-fact plot. Snyder and Swann (1978): people test a trait by asking questions that presuppose it; the target then behaves in ways that confirm it. That is hypothesis formation leaking into a contaminated Probe.

### 5. Discrimination — identify what evidence would separate them

**What it is.** Naming a datum that would be expected under one live hypothesis and surprising under the others. Diagnosticity, not vividness.

**What it is not.** More detail of the same kind. A cue that “feels revealing.” Demeanor.

**Home disciplines.** Bayesian diagnosticity, Heuer, Tetlock / superforecasting, Kelley’s triad, Strategic Use of Evidence as a *design* for a future test.

**[Evidence]** Heuer (1999): evidence is diagnostic only if it changes the **relative** likelihood of the hypotheses. An item consistent with every live hypothesis has no diagnostic value, however striking. In probability this is the likelihood ratio: P(data|H1) / P(data|H2). Posterior odds = prior odds × LR. Base rates are the prior. Kahneman and Tversky (1973, 1974): people neglect those priors when a description is representatively “the type.”

**[Evidence]** Chang, Chen, Mellers, and Tetlock (2016), *Judgment and Decision Making* 11(5): a cognitive-debiasing module (“CHAMPS KNOW”) lasting under an hour improved Brier scores **6–11%** versus control across four IARPA geopolitical tournament years. Comparison classes / base rates were the component most associated with gains. https://www.sas.upenn.edu/~baron/journal/16/16511/jdm16511.html

**[Evidence]** Mellers et al. (2015): superforecasters use finer probability grain; rounding their forecasts to the nearest 0.10 worsened Brier scores. Atanasov, Witkowski, Ungar, Mellers, and Tetlock (2020), *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* 160: accurate forecasters update frequently and in small increments; update magnitude mediated about **29%** of training’s effect on accuracy.

**[Evidence]** Hartwig, Granhag, Strömwall, and Kronkvist (2006), *Law and Human Behavior* 30: police trainees taught Strategic Use of Evidence — withhold known facts, invite an account, ask topic questions, disclose late — reached **85.4%** lie/truth accuracy versus **56.1%** untrained. They created and used **statement–evidence consistency**, not a face. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16977348/

**[Evidence]** Palena, Caso, Vrij and colleagues, Verifiability Approach meta-analysis: truth-tellers give more checkable details (g ≈ 0.42); unverifiable detail does not discriminate.

**[Inference]** Discrimination is a question asked of the future: which observation would H1 produce and H2 would not? If the answer is “none,” the current cluster is atmosphere. In the Lisbon case, “I saw that it hit you, and I didn’t like that” is high-LR for alignment. “Jane, leave me alone” is low-LR for content; it is higher-LR for unresolved private meaning. The architecture already said that: the aim is not omniscience; it is whether Deep Blue conditions remain intact.

**Failure modes.** Representativeness. Collecting more of a non-diagnostic class (Heuer Chapter 5). Using anxiety under evaluation as if it had LR ≠ 1. Confidence treated as accuracy.

### 6. Probe — deliberately acquire more information

**What it is.** An action that opens the field: a question, a silence, a reflection, a baited sketch, a rerun of A. Designed so the person can still surprise you.

**What it is not.** Interrogation. A leading question. A confession demand. A planted fact.

**Home disciplines.** PEACE, Cognitive Interview, Conversation Management, Motivational Interviewing, Padesky’s Socratic questioning, negotiation diagnostic questions.

**[Evidence]** College of Policing, *Investigative Interviewing* APP (2013, updated 2022): PEACE is Plan/Prepare → Engage/Explain → Account → Closure → Evaluation. Account starts with an open prompt, then topic probes, then challenge if needed. Challenge is planned and late. https://www.college.police.uk/app/investigation/investigative-interviewing/investigative-interviewing

**[Evidence]** Fisher and Geiselman, Cognitive Interview (1984/1992): uninterrupted free narrative, transfer of control, report everything, context reinstatement. Memon, Meissner, and Fraser (2010), *Psychology, Public Policy, and Law* 16: 46 articles, 59 effect sizes. Large increase in correct details versus control (**d = 1.20**), small increase in incorrect details (**d = 0.24**), no significant increase in confabulations, accuracy rate essentially unchanged (~1% difference). https://doi.org/10.1037/a0020518

**[Evidence]** Meissner et al. (2014), *Journal of Experimental Criminology* 10; Catlin et al. (2024) Campbell update: information-gathering styles preserve or increase true confessions and reduce false ones; accusatory / Reid-style methods increase both, including false confessions. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11292-014-9207-6

**[Evidence]** Alison, Alison, Noone, Elntib, and Christiansen (2013), ORBIT coding of 418 terrorist interviews: MI-consistent interviewer behaviour predicted adaptive suspect behaviour and evidential yield; even small amounts of accusatory or contemptuous behaviour reduced yield.

**[Evidence]** Miller and Rollnick, *Motivational Interviewing* (3rd ed., 2013): OARS. A complex reflection is a **hypothesis offer**. The speaker confirms, revises, or rejects. A higher reflection-to-question ratio is the skill, not clever questions.

**[Evidence]** Padesky (1993), “Socratic questioning: Changing minds or guiding discovery?”: ask questions the person can answer; draw attention to relevant unnoticed information; let *them* form the conclusion. If the interviewer already knows the answer, it is changing minds, not discovery. https://padesky.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/socquest.pdf

**[Evidence]** Thompson, *The Mind and Heart of the Negotiator*: questions about interests, priorities, and constraints are diagnostic for integrative agreements; most negotiators do not ask them. Reservation-price questions invite the same strategic lie under both hypotheses. Fisher, Ury, and Patton, *Getting to Yes*: ask what a position is *for*.

**[Inference]** Voss’s “calibrated questions” overlap with MI and PEACE (open what/how, labels, silence). The rest is anecdotal hostage practice. The 7-38-55 body-language claim misuses Mehrabian. Treat Voss as lore, not as a primary method.

**[Evidence]** Granhag and colleagues on the Scharff technique (friendly approach, do not press, illusion of knowing-it-all, confirmation/disconfirmation claims, downplay new information): lab HUMINT sources reveal more new information and underestimate how much they gave. The piece that maps cleanly onto ordinary Discovery is **claim-as-bait**: offer a proposition and let the person correct it. “Knowing-it-all” and planted false facts are covert tactics. Planting a false claim is Loftus contamination if the person then integrates it.

**[Inference]** Bait in the Lisbon simulation is the clean form: a candidate interpretation offered so it can be confirmed, eliminated, or deflected. It is the same act as an MI complex reflection or an MBT not-knowing sketch (“I may have this wrong — was it X or Y?”). It is not a gotcha.

**[Evidence]** Loftus and Palmer (1974): verb choice (“smashed” versus “hit”) changed speed estimates and, a week later, produced false reports of broken glass. A Probe that smuggles a fact can become the memory.

**[Evidence]** Kassin, Goldstein, and Savitsky (2003) and follow-ons: interviewers led to presume guilt chose more incriminating questions, used more pressure, and produced defensive behaviour that observers then read as guilt — even when the person was innocent.

**Training.** Open invitations, silence, reflections, delayed challenge. These transfer only if taped, coded, and coached. Miller and Mount (2001): after a two-day MI workshop, self-report soared; coded tapes moved little; client language that predicts outcome did not change. Schwalbe, Oh, and Zweben (2014): without three to four coaching sessions over six months, skills erode. Clarke and Milne (2001): PEACE was rolled out widely; skills often did not transfer; Cognitive Interview pieces were dropped as cumbersome. Powell (2008): interviewers systematically overestimate their use of open questions.

**Failure modes.** Closed-first funnel. Early challenge. Accusatory tone. Demand characteristics (Orne, 1962) and interviewer expectancy (Rosenthal). Rapport as fake warmth over a hidden agenda. Using Probe to manufacture a safety cue — already forbidden in the previous note.

### 7. Verification — test without assuming the answer

**What it is.** After a committed account or a committed observation, compare it to a withheld fact, an internal inconsistency, a later outcome, or a second observer. The test must be fail-able.

**What it is not.** “They looked shifty.” A forced reassuring display. Early cards-on-the-table that teaches a deceptive account what to accommodate.

**Home disciplines.** PEACE challenge timing, SUE, clinical guided discovery, after-action review, inter-rater reliability.

**[Evidence]** PEACE doctrine: challenge after the account is complete; treat each inconsistency as a separate objective; seek explanation; do not suggest what happened; weaker internal inconsistencies before stronger external ones; calm tone.

**[Evidence]** Walsh and Bull (2010), 142 real fraud-suspect interviews: interviewers at or above PEACE standard were far more likely to get a comprehensive account or a full admission (about 63% versus near-zero among the weakest). Planning/Preparation and Account predicted outcome. Skilled interviewing was scarce.

**[Evidence]** SUE program (Hartwig, Granhag, Strömwall, Vrij, 2005 onward): late or incremental disclosure increases statement–evidence inconsistencies in guilty mock-suspects and does not increase them in innocents. That gap is the diagnostic.

**[Evidence]** Tannenbaum and Cerasoli (2013) debrief meta-analysis, d ≈ 0.67 on performance; Keiser and Arthur (2021), 61 studies, overall d ≈ 0.79. Structured after-action review with objective media beats “how did that feel.” This is Verification-and-Update training for a team or a self.

**[Evidence]** Chorney et al. (2015): behavioral coders train to a gold-standard tape, discuss disagreements, require κ ≈ .80 before live coding, then check observer drift. About one in ten never become reliable. A cue is not a cue until it is defined, compared to a baseline, and independently seen.

**[Inference]** The previous note’s Direct versus Proxy versus Constrained safety cues are Verification design. Direct: the desired condition itself is observed. Proxy: a secondary behaviour that can mislead. Constrained: the cue answers the specific prediction, not a broader wish (“they respect me”). State Verification asks whether openness is still available, not what the hidden sentence was.

**Failure modes.** Early disclosure. Demeanor-as-test. Manufacturing the cue. Self-assessed competence after a workshop. Verification used to force Deep Blue to display itself.

### 8. Update — revise the model

**What it is.** A scored, preferably small revision of live hypotheses when the likelihood ratio is not 1. Including “this was noise.” Including “Deep Blue still holds.”

**What it is not.** A new novel that makes the new number feel inevitable. A leap that erases the prior. A refusal to move because the belief is central.

**Home disciplines.** Superforecasting, Heuer / Jervis on non-update, coder drift checks, nervous-system updating already named in the taxonomy.

**[Evidence]** Atanasov et al. (2020): frequent small updates beat rare leaps. Brier (1950) is a strictly proper score: it punishes both overconfidence and vagueness. Without a score, hindsight rewrites the prior (Fischhoff; Heuer).

**[Evidence]** Lichtenstein and Fischhoff (1980); Mellers et al. (2014); Chang et al. (2016): probability training plus outcome feedback reduces overconfidence and improves accuracy. Mandel and colleagues, testing commercial calibration courses on intelligence analysts: the main effect can be a confidence downshift, which helps the overconfident and can worsen the underconfident. Humility lectures are not automatically Update training.

**[Evidence]** Jervis (1976): beliefs central to a worldview require more diagnostic contradiction than peripheral ones. Update speed should be asymmetric by centrality, not stubbornness-as-virtue.

**[Inference]** Model Updating in the taxonomy is this stage. So is “later cues can reinterpret earlier cues” from cascade directionality. Reclassification is an Update, not an embarrassment. The Discovery reward still applies: the revision is valuable whether the news is safety or threat.

**[Hypothesis]** For this character, a short private log — “I thought X at p; happened Y” — is the lightest form of Brier. The body still has to learn the same updates. That remains an unfinished technique domain from the previous note (alarm termination, safety as conditioned inhibition). Discovery does not solve that. It supplies cleaner prediction errors for it to work on.

**Failure modes.** Consistency-seeking (Heuer). Hindsight. Multiple outs (the mentalist’s anti-update). Treating Update as a mood of open-mindedness rather than a moved number.

Verification is the reveal, the correction, or the refusal. Update revises the relational state, not the observer’s self-worth.

## VII. Training stack and reject list

The taxonomy already said: map capabilities, define scenarios, generate routines, populate routines, **only then** derive techniques. This section is not a 40-hour plan. It is what the literatures say actually moves each stage.

**What has evidence of transfer, if practiced with feedback**

1. **Detection.** VTS-style looking (art or film stills): silent inventory → “what is going on?” → “what do you see that makes you say that?” → “what more?” Dose matters. In-domain freeze-probes. A personal ethogram for one recurring environment, trained toward a second observer or a tape.
2. **Relevance linking.** Sequence review: mark A, mark B, ask whether P(B|A) exceeds P(B|~A). Conversation-analytic “why that now?” on ordinary recordings. Cognitive feedback on cue weights, not only outcomes.
3. **Interpretation.** Three lenses used as alternatives, not answers: Goffman (what performance?), Kelley (consensus / distinctiveness / consistency), process-versus-content (what is the interaction doing?). Key Assumptions Check on the first trait inference.
4. **Hypothesis.** Force at least two live hypotheses plus noise. Red-team the favorite briefly. List missing evidence. Do not require a full ACH matrix.
5. **Discrimination.** Attach a coarse probability. Name the observation that would move it. Prefer checkable detail and statement–evidence consistency over demeanor.
6. **Probe.** One open invitation or one complex reflection or one baited sketch. Silence. No evidence dump. No “why” that sounds like accusation. Treat the reply as data.
7. **Verification.** Compare the committed account or cue to a withheld fact, a later outcome, or a second observer. Constrained safety cues only. AAR on misses.
8. **Update.** Write the old number and the new one. Small moves allowed. Reclassification allowed. Do not take a commercial humility course if the problem is underconfidence.

Expertise layer, months not a weekend: deliberate, domain-specific reps with outcome-tied feedback (Ericsson, Krampe, and Tesch-Römer, 1993; Klein). There is no general “social pattern recognition” course that substitutes for time in *that* ecology. Chess chunks do not transfer. Lisbon’s ethogram does not transfer to a stranger.

**Reject list**

- Body-language seminars, METT / F.A.C.E. sold for lie detection or “reading anyone.”
- Airport *How to Read People* titles that map gestures to traits.
- NLP practitioner tracks.
- Classic police “detect deceit from demeanor” courses (near-chance plus investigator bias: Meissner and Kassin, 2002).
- Cold reading, forcing, equivoque, hot reading sold as insight.
- One-day SA or ACH workshops expected to produce field judgment.
- Voss / Cialdini as a closed system; Mehrabian 7-38-55 as a general law.
- Mindfulness aimed at rumination if the goal is *external* signal work. Open monitoring can support outward attention; MBSR is not Discovery training.
- Accusatory interrogation methods, including anything that treats confession as Verification.

## VIII. Mapping onto the existing taxonomy

Discovery does not replace the capability list. It gives several of those capabilities a shared loop.

| Existing capability | Discovery stage |
|---|---|
| Outward Attention, Social Perception, Social Telemetry | Detection |
| Pattern Recognition, Pattern Accumulation | Relevance linking |
| Bounded Inference | Interpretation |
| Signal → Model → Usable Implication | Hypothesis formation |
| Predictive Leverage (what would be true if the model is right) | Discrimination |
| Bait, Buying Time as data acquisition, questions inside Receive/Signal | Probe |
| State Verification, Direct/Proxy/Constrained safety cues | Verification |
| Model Updating, cascade reclassification | Update |
| Asymmetry-Seeking / Discovery Reward | Motive that keeps the loop outward |
| Relational Regulation (Detect → Investigate → Diagnose → Influence → Verify) | A later loop that uses Discovery, then acts |

**[Inference]** The first routine (Receive → Signal → Resolve) is not Discovery. It is a response architecture for a reachable person plus a visible incentive. Discovery may run inside Receive (notice the motive) and Signal (name it without condemnation). Resolve is aim, principle, and consequence — Strategic Abilities, not Discovery.

**[Inference]** Perceptive Detachment is a precondition. Without it, Detection still happens and then becomes an assignment. The 25 September note already isolated that: the developing character is already perceptive; the question is what the mind does with the information. Discovery is the outward use. Detachment is what stops the inward conversion.

**[Hypothesis]** The full asymmetry-seeking capability, still unfinished, is probably:

«Discovery loop (the eight stages) + reward at the information layer + predictive and behavioral leverage + a stopping rule that does not require omniscience»

The stopping rule is already in the previous note: once concealment is detected, the aim is whether Deep Blue conditions remain intact, not extraction of the hidden sentence.

## IX. What this does not settle

These remain open. They are narrower than they were.

1. **How much Discovery is portable.** Endsley, Klein, and Chase & Simon all say the same thing: expertise is ecological. How much of Jane’s speed is Lisbon-specific, and how much is a transferable loop that can be loaded with a new ethogram?
2. **The minimum Probe set for ordinary life.** PEACE, CI, MI, and SUE are institutional. The character needs versions that do not turn dinner into an interview. Bait, reflection, and calibrated interest-questions are the current candidates. They have not been scenario-tested here.
3. **Contamination in intimate Blue.** Loftus and Snyder & Swann still apply at home. The Deep Blue admission conditions include candor. A Probe that protects evidence can also protect distance. Where is the line between not contaminating and not being in the relationship?
4. **Self-Telemetry versus Social Telemetry as dual-channel Detection.** The body often fires first. That is a Detection. It is easily mis-linked (Relevance linking to “danger”) and mis-interpreted (“I am unsafe”). The cascade model is an interpretive structure, not a conditioned inhibitor. Discovery of the *external* field and Discovery of the *internal* signal can interfere.
5. **Training without an institution.** Almost every transfer finding assumes tape, coach, or scored outcomes. A solo practice will drift. What is the lightest fidelity mechanism that is not theatrical?
6. **Principles under informational advantage.** Truth without humiliation, understanding without contempt, another person’s weakness is not automatically yours to exploit — these were already listed. Discovery increases the opportunity to violate them. The capability does not contain its own ethics. Principles stay a separate layer.
7. **Alarm termination.** Cleaner prediction errors help safety learning. They do not, by themselves, end an alarm episode. That technique domain remains open.

## X. Distilled invariant

Discovery is large. It is not shapeless.

It is not mentalism. It is not body language. It is not “how do I read people?” It is not a theatrical craft.

It is the staged acquisition of previously unavailable information about a situation — people included, not required — by noticing, linking, interpreting, holding rivals, naming what would split them, asking in a way that can still surprise you, testing without inventing the answer, and moving the model.

The Jane/Lisbon example remains the cleanest specimen:

«event → subsequent reaction → contingency against baseline → probe that can fail → update»

The disciplines that improve that loop are observational method, situational awareness, sequential analysis, investigative and clinical interviewing, intelligence analysis of incentives and rivals, and scored hypothesis revision.

The disciplines that pretend to replace it are face-reading, cue dictionaries, and guaranteed-outcome stagecraft.

The core stays the founder’s:

> Notice more. Connect better. Interpret cautiously. Test intelligently. Update quickly.
